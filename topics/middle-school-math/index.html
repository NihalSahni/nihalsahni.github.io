MATH

MIDDLE SCHOOL · 77 CONCEPTS

Every Math concept in the middle school Science Bowl study set (grades 6-8), with a short definition for each. Read through them here, then drill them as flashcards or practise real Math questions.

Quadratic Equations

Form ax² + bx + c = 0. Quadratic formula: x = (−b ± √(b²−4ac)) / 2a. Discriminant Δ = b²−4ac: Δ > 0 → two real roots; Δ = 0 → one real root; Δ < 0 → two complex roots. Vertex at x = −b/2a. Sum of roots = −b/a; product of roots = c/a.

Logarithms

log_b(x) = y ↔ bʸ = x. ln(x) = log_e(x). Rules: log(ab) = log a + log b; log(a/b) = log a − log b; log(aⁿ) = n·log a; log_b(1) = 0; log_b(b) = 1. Change of base: log_b(x) = ln(x)/ln(b). Inverse of exponential: b^(log_b(x)) = x.

Trigonometry

SOH-CAH-TOA: sin = opp/hyp, cos = adj/hyp, tan = opp/adj. Pythagorean identity: sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. Other identities: 1 + tan²θ = sec²θ; 1 + cot²θ = csc²θ. Law of Sines: a/sinA = b/sinB. Law of Cosines: c² = a² + b² − 2ab·cosC. Radians: 180° = π rad.

Derivatives

Instantaneous rate of change. Power rule: d/dx[xⁿ] = nxⁿ⁻¹. Product: (uv)' = u'v + uv'. Quotient: (u/v)' = (u'v − uv')/v². Chain rule: d/dx[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x))·g'(x). d/dx[sin x] = cos x; d/dx[eˣ] = eˣ; d/dx[ln x] = 1/x. Critical points where f'(x) = 0 or undefined.

Integrals

Antiderivative / area under curve. Power rule: ∫xⁿdx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n+1) + C (n ≠ −1). Fundamental Theorem: ∫[a,b] f(x)dx = F(b) − F(a). Key: ∫eˣdx = eˣ + C; ∫(1/x)dx = ln|x| + C; ∫cos x dx = sin x + C; ∫sin x dx = −cos x + C. Integration by substitution and by parts.

Probability & Combinations

P(A) = favorable / total. P(A and B) = P(A)·P(B) for independent events. P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A∩B). Combinations C(n,r) = n!/[r!(n−r)!] — order doesn't matter. Permutations P(n,r) = n!/(n−r)! — order matters. Conditional: P(A|B) = P(A∩B)/P(B).

Sequences & Series

Arithmetic: aₙ = a₁ + (n−1)d; sum Sₙ = n(a₁+aₙ)/2. Geometric: aₙ = a₁·rⁿ⁻¹; sum Sₙ = a₁(1−rⁿ)/(1−r). Infinite geometric series: S = a₁/(1−r) if |r| < 1. Fibonacci: aₙ = aₙ₋₁ + aₙ₋₂ (1,1,2,3,5,8,13,…). Telescoping series: most terms cancel.

Complex Numbers

i = √(−1); i² = −1; i³ = −i; i⁴ = 1. Form z = a + bi. Addition: add real and imaginary parts. Multiplication: FOIL (use i² = −1). Modulus |z| = √(a²+b²). Conjugate: z̄ = a − bi; z·z̄ = |z|². Euler: e^(iθ) = cosθ + i sinθ. Division: multiply by conjugate over conjugate.

Statistics

Mean μ = Σx/n. Median: middle value. Mode: most frequent. Variance σ² = Σ(x−μ)²/n. Standard deviation σ = √(variance). Normal distribution: 68–95–99.7 rule (1σ, 2σ, 3σ). Correlation r ∈ [−1, +1]. Linear regression: line of best fit. Outliers can skew mean but not median.

Matrices

Rectangular arrays of numbers. Matrix multiplication: rows × columns (NOT commutative). det of 2×2 [a b; c d] = ad − bc. Inverse A⁻¹ exists iff det ≠ 0. Identity matrix I: A·I = A. Solve systems via Gaussian elimination or Cramer's Rule. Eigenvalue λ: Av = λv (eigenvector v).

Vectors

Quantities with magnitude and direction. Component form: v = ⟨a, b, c⟩. Magnitude |v| = √(a²+b²+c²). Dot product: u·v = |u||v|cosθ = Σuᵢvᵢ (scalar; zero if perpendicular). Cross product: u×v is perpendicular to both; |u×v| = |u||v|sinθ. Unit vector: v/|v|.

Limits & Continuity

lim_{x→a} f(x) = L if f(x) approaches L as x → a (from both sides). L'Hôpital's Rule: for 0/0 or ∞/∞ indeterminate forms, lim f/g = lim f'/g'. Continuity at a: f(a) defined, limit exists, and lim_{x→a} f(x) = f(a). Squeeze theorem: if g(x) ≤ f(x) ≤ h(x) and g,h → L, then f → L.

Number Theory

Prime numbers have exactly two factors: 1 and themselves. Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic: every integer > 1 has a unique prime factorization. GCD: greatest common divisor; LCM: least common multiple; GCD × LCM = product of two numbers. Modular arithmetic: a ≡ b (mod n) means n divides (a−b). Euler's totient function φ(n).

Conic Sections

Circle: (x−h)² + (y−k)² = r². Ellipse: x²/a² + y²/b² = 1 (a > b; foci at ±c where c² = a²−b²). Parabola: y = ax² + bx + c; focus/directrix definition. Hyperbola: x²/a² − y²/b² = 1; asymptotes y = ±(b/a)x. All are cross-sections of a double cone.

Polynomial Division

Long division of polynomials mirrors long division of integers. Synthetic division: shortcut when dividing by (x − c). Remainder Theorem: dividing f(x) by (x − c) gives remainder f(c). Factor Theorem: (x − c) is a factor of f(x) iff f(c) = 0. Rational Root Theorem: rational roots of aₙxⁿ + … + a₀ are ±(factors of a₀)/(factors of aₙ).

Functions & Transformations

Domain: set of valid inputs. Range: set of outputs. Composition: (f∘g)(x) = f(g(x)). Inverse: f⁻¹ exists if f is one-to-one (passes horizontal line test); f(f⁻¹(x)) = x. Transformations: f(x) + k (vertical shift), f(x + h) (horizontal shift), af(x) (vertical stretch), f(bx) (horizontal compression).

Binomial Theorem

(a + b)ⁿ = Σ C(n,k) aⁿ⁻ᵏ bᵏ for k = 0 to n. The kth term (starting from 1) = C(n,k−1)·aⁿ⁻⁽ᵏ⁻¹⁾·bᵏ⁻¹. Pascal's triangle rows give the coefficients. C(n,k) = n!/[k!(n−k)!]. Used in probability distributions (binomial distribution) and approximations.

Set Theory

Set: unordered collection of distinct elements. Union A ∪ B: elements in A or B. Intersection A ∩ B: elements in both. Complement Aᶜ: elements not in A. Empty set ∅. Subset: A ⊆ B if every element of A is in B. |A ∪ B| = |A| + |B| − |A ∩ B| (inclusion-exclusion). Venn diagrams visualize set relationships.

Linear Programming

Optimize a linear objective function subject to linear inequality constraints. Feasible region: all points satisfying all constraints (convex polygon). Optimal solution always occurs at a vertex (corner point) of the feasible region. Used in operations research, resource allocation, and supply chain optimization.

Differential Equations

Equations involving a function and its derivatives. Separable ODEs: dy/dx = f(x)g(y) → separate and integrate. Exponential growth/decay: dy/dt = ky → y = y₀eᵏᵗ. Second-order linear ODEs model springs (y'' + ky = 0 → y = A cos√k·t + B sin√k·t). Initial conditions determine particular solutions.

Polar Coordinates

Points described by (r, θ): r = distance from origin, θ = angle from positive x-axis. Conversion: x = r cosθ, y = r sinθ; r = √(x²+y²), θ = arctan(y/x). Circle r = a. Rose curves: r = a cos(nθ). Cardioid: r = a(1 + cosθ). Area in polar: A = ½∫r²dθ.

Rational Functions

f(x) = p(x)/q(x) where p, q are polynomials. Vertical asymptote where q(x) = 0 (and p(x) ≠ 0). Horizontal asymptote: if deg(p) < deg(q) → y = 0; deg(p) = deg(q) → y = ratio of leading coefficients; deg(p) > deg(q) → oblique asymptote. Holes where both p and q have a common factor.

Absolute Value & Inequalities

|x| = x if x ≥ 0; −x if x < 0. |x| < a means −a < x < a. |x| > a means x > a or x < −a. Triangle inequality: |a + b| ≤ |a| + |b|. Solving quadratic inequalities: factor, find roots, test intervals. Sign chart method: track sign changes at roots and asymptotes.

Parametric Equations

Curve defined by x = f(t) and y = g(t) where t is a parameter. Eliminate the parameter to get Cartesian form. Derivative: dy/dx = (dy/dt)/(dx/dt). Arc length: L = ∫√[(dx/dt)²+(dy/dt)²] dt. Circle: x = r cosθ, y = r sinθ. Projectile: x = v₀cosθ·t, y = v₀sinθ·t − ½gt².

Graph Theory

Graphs consist of vertices (nodes) and edges. Degree of vertex: number of edges connected. Euler path: visits every edge exactly once (exists if exactly 0 or 2 odd-degree vertices). Euler circuit: Euler path starting and ending at same vertex (0 odd-degree vertices). Trees: connected graphs with no cycles; n vertices, n−1 edges.

Taylor & Maclaurin Series

Taylor series: f(x) = Σ f⁽ⁿ⁾(a)/n! · (x−a)ⁿ; approximates a function around point a. Maclaurin series: Taylor series centered at 0. Common Maclaurin series: eˣ = 1 + x + x²/2! + x³/3! + …; sin x = x − x³/3! + x⁵/5! − …; cos x = 1 − x²/2! + x⁴/4! − …; ln(1+x) = x − x²/2 + x³/3 − … (|x| ≤ 1). Radius of convergence determined by ratio test. Truncating gives polynomial approximations.

Integration Techniques

U-substitution: reverse of chain rule; let u = g(x), du = g'(x)dx. Integration by parts: ∫u dv = uv − ∫v du (choose u by LIATE: Logarithm, Inverse trig, Algebraic, Trig, Exponential). Trigonometric substitution: for √(a²−x²) use x = a sinθ; for √(a²+x²) use x = a tanθ; for √(x²−a²) use x = a secθ. Partial fractions: decompose rational functions into simpler fractions before integrating. Tabular integration: efficient for repeated integration by parts.

Multivariable Calculus

Functions of multiple variables f(x, y, z). Partial derivatives: ∂f/∂x holds other variables constant. Gradient ∇f = (∂f/∂x, ∂f/∂y, ∂f/∂z): points in direction of steepest ascent. Directional derivative: rate of change in direction of unit vector û. Double integrals: ∫∫f(x,y) dA; can switch to polar coordinates. Triple integrals: volume and mass calculations. Line integrals: ∫F·dr along a curve. Green's, Stokes', and divergence theorems connect line/surface/volume integrals.

Proof Techniques

Direct proof: assume hypothesis, show conclusion follows logically. Proof by contrapositive: prove 'not Q → not P' instead of 'P → Q'. Proof by contradiction: assume negation of conclusion, derive contradiction. Mathematical induction: base case (n=1 true) + inductive step (if n=k true, then n=k+1 true) → true for all positive integers. Strong induction: assume all cases up to k. Proof by cases: exhaust all possible cases. Counterexample: single example that disproves a universal claim.

Probability Distributions

Discrete distributions: Binomial P(X=k) = C(n,k)pᵏ(1−p)ⁿ⁻ᵏ; μ = np, σ² = np(1−p). Poisson P(X=k) = e^(−λ)λᵏ/k!; μ = σ² = λ (rare events in interval). Continuous distributions: Normal (bell curve; 68-95-99.7 rule). Exponential: time between Poisson events; memoryless property. Uniform: equal probability on interval. Central Limit Theorem: sample means approach normal distribution regardless of population distribution as n → ∞.

Number Bases & Modular Arithmetic

Base-2 (binary): digits 0,1; used in computers. Base-8 (octal): digits 0–7. Base-16 (hexadecimal): digits 0–9 and A–F. Conversion: repeatedly divide by base, collect remainders. Modular arithmetic: a ≡ b (mod n) means n | (a−b). Arithmetic mod n: (a+b) mod n, (ab) mod n. Fermat's Little Theorem: if p prime, aᵖ ≡ a (mod p). Chinese Remainder Theorem: system of congruences with coprime moduli has unique solution mod product. Applications: cryptography, hashing, calendar calculations.

Combinatorics (Advanced)

Inclusion-exclusion principle: |A∪B∪C| = |A|+|B|+|C| − |A∩B| − |A∩C| − |B∩C| + |A∩B∩C|. Stars and bars: distributing n identical objects into k distinct bins = C(n+k−1, k−1). Derangements: permutations with no fixed point; D(n) = n!(1 − 1/1! + 1/2! − … + (−1)ⁿ/n!). Catalan numbers: Cₙ = C(2n,n)/(n+1); count valid parenthesizations and paths. Pigeonhole principle: if n+1 items in n containers, at least one container has ≥2 items.

Euler's Formula & Complex Analysis

Euler's formula: e^(iθ) = cosθ + i sinθ. Euler's identity: e^(iπ) + 1 = 0. De Moivre's theorem: (cosθ + i sinθ)ⁿ = cos(nθ) + i sin(nθ). Polar form of complex number: z = r e^(iθ) = r(cosθ + i sinθ). Multiplication in polar form: multiply moduli, add arguments. Roots of unity: solutions to zⁿ = 1 are equally spaced on unit circle. nth roots: n solutions, spaced 2π/n apart. Fourier series uses complex exponentials as basis functions.

Linear Algebra (Eigenvalues)

Eigenvalue equation: Av = λv (A is matrix, λ is eigenvalue, v is eigenvector). Find eigenvalues: solve det(A − λI) = 0 (characteristic polynomial). Eigenvectors: solve (A − λI)v = 0. Diagonalization: A = PDP⁻¹ where D is diagonal matrix of eigenvalues, P has eigenvectors as columns. Symmetric matrices have real eigenvalues and orthogonal eigenvectors. Applications: principal component analysis (PCA), differential equations, Google PageRank, quantum mechanics.

Recurrence Relations

Equations defining sequences in terms of previous terms. Linear recurrences with constant coefficients: aₙ = c₁aₙ₋₁ + c₂aₙ₋₂ + … Characteristic equation method: substitute aₙ = rⁿ to get characteristic polynomial; roots r give solution. Fibonacci: aₙ = aₙ₋₁ + aₙ₋₂; closed form (Binet's formula): Fₙ = (φⁿ − ψⁿ)/√5 where φ = (1+√5)/2 (golden ratio). Generating functions: represent sequence as power series coefficients to solve recurrences.

Hyperbolic Functions

Defined using exponentials: sinh x = (eˣ − e⁻ˣ)/2; cosh x = (eˣ + e⁻ˣ)/2; tanh x = sinh x / cosh x. Identity: cosh²x − sinh²x = 1. Derivatives: d/dx[sinh x] = cosh x; d/dx[cosh x] = sinh x. Inverse: arcsinh x = ln(x + √(x²+1)); arccosh x = ln(x + √(x²−1)) (x ≥ 1). Applications: catenary curve (hanging chain); special relativity (rapidity); solutions to Laplace's equation.

Game Theory

Mathematical study of strategic decision-making. Nash equilibrium: no player can improve their payoff by unilaterally changing strategy; can have multiple or no pure-strategy equilibria. Prisoner's dilemma: individually rational choices lead to collectively suboptimal outcomes. Dominant strategy: best regardless of opponent's choice. Zero-sum games: one player's gain = other's loss (solved by minimax theorem). Mixed strategies: probabilistic choices. Applications: economics, evolutionary biology (evolutionarily stable strategies), political science.

Improper Integrals & Convergence

Improper integral: infinite limits or discontinuous integrand. Type 1 (infinite limits): ∫[a,∞) f(x)dx = lim_{t→∞} ∫[a,t] f(x)dx. Type 2 (discontinuity): ∫[a,b] f(x)dx where f is discontinuous at endpoint. Comparison test: if 0 ≤ f(x) ≤ g(x) and ∫g converges, so does ∫f. p-integral: ∫[1,∞) x^(−p)dx converges iff p > 1. Gamma function: Γ(n) = ∫[0,∞) x^(n−1)e^(−x)dx = (n−1)! for positive integers.

Boolean Algebra & Logic Gates

Boolean algebra uses values 0 (false) and 1 (true). Operations: AND (A·B), OR (A+B), NOT (A'). De Morgan's Laws: (A·B)' = A'+B'; (A+B)' = A'·B'. Logic gates: AND, OR, NOT, NAND, NOR, XOR (A⊕B = A'B + AB'). Truth tables enumerate all input combinations. Sum of products (SOP) and product of sums (POS) forms. Karnaugh maps simplify Boolean expressions. Applications: digital circuit design, computer architecture, cryptography.

Numerical Methods

Algorithms for approximating solutions to mathematical problems. Newton-Raphson method: xₙ₊₁ = xₙ − f(xₙ)/f'(xₙ); fast convergence (quadratic) for root-finding. Bisection method: bracket root, halve interval; slower but guaranteed. Euler's method: numerical ODE solving; yₙ₊₁ = yₙ + h·f(xₙ, yₙ). Runge-Kutta (RK4): more accurate ODE solver. Trapezoidal rule and Simpson's rule: numerical integration. Error analysis: truncation error (method) vs roundoff error (finite precision).

Fractions, Decimals & Percents

Fraction: part of a whole (numerator/denominator). To convert fraction to decimal: divide numerator by denominator. Percent: parts per hundred; convert to decimal by dividing by 100 (75% = 0.75). Percent of a number: multiply (30% of 80 = 0.30 × 80 = 24). Operations with fractions: to add/subtract, find a common denominator; to multiply, multiply straight across; to divide, multiply by the reciprocal.

Ratios & Proportions

A ratio compares two quantities (3:4 or 3/4). A proportion is an equation stating two ratios are equal (3/4 = 6/8). Cross-multiplication: if a/b = c/d then ad = bc. Unit rate: ratio with denominator 1 (60 miles per hour). Scale factor: ratio used in scale drawings and similar figures. Direct variation: y = kx; as x increases, y increases proportionally.

Algebra Basics

Variable: a letter representing an unknown value. Expression: combination of numbers, variables, and operations (3x + 2). Equation: two expressions set equal (3x + 2 = 11). Solving: use inverse operations to isolate the variable. Order of operations: PEMDAS (Parentheses, Exponents, Multiplication/Division, Addition/Subtraction). Distributive property: a(b + c) = ab + ac.

Geometry (Area & Perimeter)

Perimeter: sum of all side lengths. Area formulas: rectangle = length × width; triangle = ½ × base × height; parallelogram = base × height; trapezoid = ½(b₁ + b₂) × h; circle = πr². Circumference of circle = 2πr = πd. Volume: rectangular prism = l × w × h; cylinder = πr²h; cone = ⅓πr²h; sphere = 4/3πr³. Surface area = sum of all face areas.

Exponents & Scientific Notation

Exponent rules: xᵃ × xᵇ = xᵃ⁺ᵇ; xᵃ / xᵇ = xᵃ⁻ᵇ; (xᵃ)ᵇ = xᵃᵇ; x⁰ = 1; x⁻ⁿ = 1/xⁿ. Scientific notation: a × 10ⁿ where 1 ≤ a < 10. To multiply in scientific notation: multiply the coefficients and add exponents. To add/subtract: first make the exponents equal. Examples: 3×10⁴ × 2×10³ = 6×10⁷.

Probability (Basic)

Probability = number of favorable outcomes / total number of outcomes. Range: 0 (impossible) to 1 (certain). Complementary events: P(A) + P(not A) = 1. Independent events: P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B). Mutually exclusive events: P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B). Experimental probability: based on trials; theoretical probability: based on perfect conditions.

Statistics & Data (Basic)

Mean (average): sum of values ÷ number of values. Median: middle value when data is ordered (average of two middle values if even count). Mode: most frequently occurring value. Range: maximum − minimum. Interquartile range (IQR): Q3 − Q1. Outlier: a data point that is much higher or lower than most. Bar graphs, histograms, box plots, and scatter plots display data.

Integers & Number Properties

Integers include positive whole numbers, negative whole numbers, and zero. Adding integers: same sign → add and keep sign; different signs → subtract and keep the sign of the larger absolute value. Multiplying/dividing: same signs → positive result; different signs → negative result. Absolute value |x|: distance from zero on a number line; always non-negative.

Linear Equations & Graphing

Linear equation: y = mx + b (slope-intercept form). Slope (m) = rise/run = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁); represents rate of change. Y-intercept (b): where line crosses the y-axis. Positive slope: line goes up left to right. Negative slope: line goes down. Horizontal line: slope = 0. Vertical line: undefined slope. Two lines are parallel if they have equal slopes.

Place Value & Number Systems

The value of a digit depends on its position (place) in a number. In base 10: ones, tens, hundreds, thousands, … to the left of the decimal; tenths, hundredths, thousandths, … to the right. Expanded form: 3,472 = 3×1000 + 4×100 + 7×10 + 2×1. Rounding: look at the digit to the right of the rounding place; if ≥5, round up; if <5, round down. Comparing decimals: align decimal points and compare digit by digit. The number line shows all real numbers in order; negative numbers are to the left of zero.

Negative Numbers & Absolute Value

Negative numbers are less than zero; used for temperatures below freezing, debt, sea level depths. On a number line, negatives are left of zero. Absolute value |x|: the distance from zero; always non-negative. |-5| = |5| = 5. Adding: same signs → add and keep sign; different signs → subtract and keep sign of larger absolute value. Subtracting: add the opposite. Multiplying/Dividing: same signs → positive; opposite signs → negative. Ordering integers: −10 < −3 < 0 < 5 < 12.

Order of Operations

PEMDAS (Please Excuse My Dear Aunt Sally): Parentheses first, then Exponents, then Multiplication and Division (left to right), then Addition and Subtraction (left to right). Also called BODMAS (Brackets, Orders, Division/Multiplication, Addition/Subtraction). Example: 3 + 4 × 2² − (8 ÷ 4) = 3 + 4 × 4 − 2 = 3 + 16 − 2 = 17. Common mistakes: forgetting that multiplication and division have equal priority (left to right), and same for addition and subtraction. Brackets/parentheses always evaluated first, innermost first.

Factors, Multiples & Divisibility

Factor: a number that divides evenly into another (factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12). Multiple: result of multiplying a number by an integer (multiples of 5: 5, 10, 15, 20…). GCF (Greatest Common Factor): largest factor shared by two numbers (GCF of 12 and 18 is 6). LCM (Least Common Multiple): smallest number divisible by both (LCM of 4 and 6 is 12). GCF × LCM = product of the two numbers. Divisibility rules: divisible by 2 (even), 3 (digit sum divisible by 3), 4 (last two digits), 5 (ends in 0 or 5), 6 (divisible by 2 and 3), 9 (digit sum divisible by 9), 10 (ends in 0).

Coordinate Plane

Two perpendicular number lines (x-axis horizontal, y-axis vertical) intersecting at the origin (0,0). Points written as ordered pairs (x, y). Four quadrants: I (+, +), II (−, +), III (−, −), IV (+, −). Plotting points: move x units horizontally, y units vertically from origin. Distance between two points: d = √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²]. Midpoint: ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2). Reflections: across x-axis → (x, −y); across y-axis → (−x, y); across origin → (−x, −y). Used for graphing equations and geometric transformations.

Inequalities

Inequality symbols: < (less than), > (greater than), ≤ (less than or equal), ≥ (greater than or equal), ≠ (not equal). Solving: same as equations, BUT when multiplying or dividing both sides by a negative number, flip the inequality sign. Graphing on a number line: open circle for < or > (endpoint not included); closed circle for ≤ or ≥ (included). Compound inequalities: AND (intersection; 'between' — both conditions must be met); OR (union; either condition). Example: −2 < x ≤ 5 means x is greater than −2 AND less than or equal to 5.

Pythagorean Theorem

In a right triangle, the square of the hypotenuse (longest side, opposite the right angle) equals the sum of the squares of the other two legs: a² + b² = c². Used to find the length of any side if two sides are known. Example: a=3, b=4 → c=√(9+16)=√25=5 (3-4-5 is a Pythagorean triple). Other common triples: 5-12-13, 8-15-17. Also used to determine if a triangle is right-angled: if a² + b² = c², it's a right triangle. Converse: if a² + b² ≠ c², not a right triangle. Can find distances in the coordinate plane.

Angle Relationships

Complementary angles: sum to 90°. Supplementary angles: sum to 180°. Vertical angles: formed by intersecting lines; they are equal. Adjacent angles: share a vertex and a side. Straight angle = 180°. Right angle = 90°. Acute angle < 90°. Obtuse angle: between 90° and 180°. Transversal crossing parallel lines creates: corresponding angles (equal), alternate interior angles (equal), alternate exterior angles (equal), co-interior/consecutive interior angles (supplementary = 180°). Triangle angle sum = 180°. Exterior angle of triangle = sum of two non-adjacent interior angles.

Triangles

Classified by sides: equilateral (all sides equal, all angles 60°), isosceles (two equal sides, two equal base angles), scalene (no equal sides). Classified by angles: acute (all angles < 90°), right (one 90° angle), obtuse (one angle > 90°). Triangle inequality: sum of any two sides > third side. Area = ½ × base × height. Perimeter = sum of all three sides. Similar triangles: same shape, proportional sides (AA, SAS, SSS similarity). Congruent triangles: same shape and size (SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS congruence). Largest angle opposite longest side.

Proportional Relationships & Unit Rate

A proportional relationship: y = kx where k is the constant of proportionality (unit rate). On a graph: a straight line through the origin. k = y/x for any point. Unit rate: ratio expressed as a quantity per one unit (speed in miles per hour, price per pound, heart beats per minute). Scale factor: ratio of corresponding measurements in similar figures. To solve proportions: cross-multiply. Direct proportion: as x increases, y increases proportionally. Inverse proportion: as x increases, y decreases (xy = k). Percent proportion: part/whole = percent/100.

Percent Applications

Percent change = (new − old) / old × 100%. Percent increase: positive change. Percent decrease: negative change (% decrease = (old − new)/old × 100%). Simple interest: I = Prt (principal × rate × time in years). Discount: subtract percent of original price. Markup: add percent of cost to cost. Tax/tip: add percent of price. Finding original amount after percent change: original = new amount / (1 ± percent as decimal). Sales tax: total = price × (1 + tax rate). Example: item costs $80, 25% off: discount = $20, sale price = $60.

Geometry: Volume & Surface Area

Volume: rectangular prism = l × w × h; cube = s³; cylinder = πr²h; cone = ⅓πr²h; sphere = (4/3)πr³; pyramid = ⅓ × base area × height. Surface area: rectangular prism = 2(lw + lh + wh); cube = 6s²; cylinder = 2πr² + 2πrh; sphere = 4πr². Net: a flat shape that can be folded into a 3D figure; used to visualize and calculate surface area. Cavalieri's Principle: two solids with equal height and equal cross-sectional areas at every level have equal volumes. Units of volume: cm³, m³, liters (1 L = 1000 cm³).

Transformations

Translation: slides a figure along a vector (a, b) — adds a to x-coordinates, b to y-coordinates. Rotation: turns a figure around a center point by a given angle (90°, 180°, 270° CW or CCW). Reflection: flips a figure over a line of reflection. Dilation: enlarges or reduces a figure by a scale factor k (k>1 enlargement; 0<k<1 reduction). Rigid transformations (isometries): translations, rotations, reflections — preserve size and shape. Non-rigid: dilations — preserve shape but not size. Congruent figures: related by rigid transformations. Similar figures: related by dilations (and rigid transformations).

Systems of Equations

Two or more equations solved simultaneously. Graphing method: find intersection point of lines. Substitution method: solve one equation for a variable, substitute into the other. Elimination (addition) method: add or subtract equations to eliminate a variable. Three possible outcomes: one solution (lines intersect at a point — consistent and independent), no solution (parallel lines — inconsistent), infinitely many solutions (same line — consistent and dependent). Applications: finding two unknown quantities simultaneously (mixture problems, age problems, distance-rate-time problems).

Functions (Basic)

A function: a rule that assigns exactly one output (y) to each input (x). Represented as f(x). Vertical line test: if any vertical line crosses the graph more than once, it's NOT a function. Domain: set of all valid inputs (x-values). Range: set of all outputs (y-values). Ways to represent functions: tables, graphs, equations, mapping diagrams. Linear function: f(x) = mx + b. Quadratic function: f(x) = ax² + bx + c (parabola). Increasing: output increases as input increases. Decreasing: output decreases as input increases.

Data & Graphs

Bar graph: compares categories using bars of different heights. Line graph: shows change over time; good for trends. Pie chart (circle graph): shows parts of a whole as sectors (percentages must sum to 100%). Histogram: bar chart for continuous data organized in intervals/bins; no gaps between bars. Scatter plot: shows relationship between two quantitative variables; each point represents one data pair. Line of best fit (trend line): drawn through scatter plot to show general trend. Positive correlation: both variables increase together. Negative correlation: one increases, other decreases. No correlation: no pattern.

Measures of Center & Spread

Measures of center: mean (sum ÷ count; affected by outliers), median (middle value when ordered; resistant to outliers), mode (most frequent value; can have multiple or none). Measures of spread: range (max − min), interquartile range IQR = Q3 − Q1 (middle 50% of data; resistant to outliers), mean absolute deviation (MAD = average distance from mean). Standard deviation: average distance from the mean (larger → more spread out). Outlier: a value more than 1.5 × IQR below Q1 or above Q3. Box plot shows min, Q1, median, Q3, max.

Scientific Notation (Detail)

Scientific notation: a × 10ⁿ where 1 ≤ a < 10. Very large: 4,300,000 = 4.3 × 10⁶. Very small: 0.00057 = 5.7 × 10⁻⁴. To multiply: multiply coefficients and add exponents (3×10⁴ × 2×10³ = 6×10⁷). To divide: divide coefficients and subtract exponents. To add/subtract: must convert to same power of 10 first. Applications in science: speed of light = 3×10⁸ m/s; mass of electron = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg; distance to Andromeda galaxy = 2.5×10⁶ light-years. Calculators display scientific notation as '6.02E23' for 6.02×10²³.

Simple & Compound Interest

Simple interest: I = Prt; total amount A = P(1 + rt). P = principal, r = annual interest rate (decimal), t = time (years). Example: $1000 at 5% for 3 years: I = 1000 × 0.05 × 3 = $150; total = $1150. Compound interest: interest is calculated on principal AND previously earned interest. Formula: A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt) where n = number of times compounded per year. Continuously compounded: A = Pe^(rt). Compound interest grows faster than simple interest. Rule of 72: approximate years to double money = 72 / interest rate (%).

Square Roots & Cube Roots

Square root: √x = y means y² = x. Perfect squares: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144, 169, 196, 225. √72 = 6√2 (simplify by factoring out largest perfect square). Cube root: ∛x = y means y³ = x. Perfect cubes: 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 216, 343. Irrational numbers: square roots of non-perfect squares (√2 ≈ 1.414, √3 ≈ 1.732); cannot be expressed as a fraction; non-terminating, non-repeating decimals. Estimating square roots: √50 is between √49=7 and √64=8, closer to 7 since 50 is closer to 49.

Proportions & Similar Figures

Similar figures: same shape, different size; corresponding angles are equal; corresponding sides are proportional. Scale factor: ratio of corresponding sides (e.g., scale factor of 3 means all lengths tripled, areas × 9, volumes × 27). To find missing side in similar figures: set up proportion (a/b = c/d) and cross-multiply. Scale drawings: maps, blueprints, models use a scale ratio (e.g., 1 cm = 50 km). Indirect measurement: use similar triangles to measure heights/distances you cannot measure directly (e.g., shadow method to find tree height).

Probability: Compound Events

Compound event: involves two or more simple events. Independent events: outcome of one does not affect the other (flipping two coins). P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B) for independent events. Dependent events: outcome of one affects the other (drawing cards without replacement). P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B|A) (conditional probability). P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B) (addition rule). Complementary: P(not A) = 1 − P(A). Sample space: list all possible outcomes. Tree diagrams and tables help organize compound probability problems.

Number Patterns & Sequences

Arithmetic sequence: each term found by adding a constant (common difference d). aₙ = a₁ + (n−1)d. Example: 3, 7, 11, 15 (d=4). Sum of n terms: Sₙ = n(a₁+aₙ)/2. Geometric sequence: each term found by multiplying by a constant (common ratio r). aₙ = a₁ × r^(n−1). Example: 2, 6, 18, 54 (r=3). Sum of geometric series: Sₙ = a₁(1−rⁿ)/(1−r). Fibonacci sequence: 1, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13 (each term is sum of previous two). Recognizing patterns: look for common differences, ratios, or more complex rules.

Geometry: Circles

Circle: set of all points equidistant from center. Radius (r): center to edge. Diameter (d = 2r): across center. Circumference C = 2πr = πd. Area A = πr². π ≈ 3.14159. Arc: part of the circumference; arc length = (central angle/360°) × 2πr. Sector: pie-slice region; area = (central angle/360°) × πr². Chord: segment connecting two points on circle. Tangent: line touching circle at exactly one point. Inscribed angle: vertex on circle; inscribed angle = ½ × intercepted arc. Central angle = intercepted arc. Semicircle: 180°.

Rational & Irrational Numbers

Rational numbers: can be expressed as a fraction p/q where p and q are integers and q ≠ 0; includes all integers, fractions, terminating decimals (0.75), and repeating decimals (0.333… = 1/3). Irrational numbers: cannot be expressed as a fraction; non-terminating, non-repeating decimals (√2 = 1.41421…; π = 3.14159…). Real numbers: all rational and irrational numbers combined. Converting repeating decimal to fraction: let x = 0.333…; 10x = 3.333…; 10x − x = 3; 9x = 3; x = 1/3. √(perfect square) is rational; √(non-perfect square) is irrational.

Algebra: Solving Multi-Step Equations

Goal: isolate the variable using inverse operations. Steps: 1) Use distributive property to remove parentheses. 2) Combine like terms on each side. 3) Use addition/subtraction to get variable terms on one side. 4) Use multiplication/division to solve for variable. Variables on both sides: collect all variable terms on one side, constants on the other. Example: 3(x + 2) = 2x + 11 → 3x + 6 = 2x + 11 → x = 5. Always check your answer by substituting back into the original equation. Equations with no solution (0=5) or infinite solutions (5=5).

Scale Drawings & Measurement

Scale drawing: represents a real object proportionally. Scale: written as ratio (1:100) or with units (1 cm = 10 m). To find actual size: multiply drawing measurement by scale factor. To find drawing size: divide actual size by scale factor. Unit conversions (customary): 12 inches = 1 foot; 3 feet = 1 yard; 5,280 feet = 1 mile. Unit conversions (metric): kilo (×1000), hecto (×100), deka (×10), base, deci (÷10), centi (÷100), milli (÷1000). Converting metric: move decimal point. Converting between systems: 1 inch = 2.54 cm; 1 kg ≈ 2.2 lb; 1 mile ≈ 1.61 km.

Sampling & Statistics

Population: entire group being studied. Sample: subset of population used to make inferences. Random sample: every member has an equal chance of being selected — reduces bias. Sampling methods: simple random (lottery/random number), stratified (divide into subgroups, sample proportionally), systematic (every nth person), convenience (easiest available — most biased). Bias: systematic error that skews results. Sample size: larger samples → more accurate estimates. Margin of error: indicates how much sample results may differ from true population value. Surveys, experiments, and observational studies are different ways to collect data.
