HIGH SCHOOL CHEMISTRY QUESTIONS

GRADES 9-12 · 15 REAL QUESTIONS · ANSWERS EXPLAINED

Chemistry is one of the six Science Bowl subjects. At the high school level it covers atomic structure, bonding, periodic trends, stoichiometry, acids and bases, thermodynamics and organic chemistry. Below are 15 real questions from official National Science Bowl sets, each with its answer and a short explanation of why it's right. There are 220 high school chemistry questions in all on this site. When you're ready, practise them against the clock or review the high school chemistry concepts.

QUESTION 1 · SHORT ANSWER

What is the name of the principle that states that equal volumes of gasses at the same temperature and pressure contain the same number of particles?

ANSWER Avogadro's principle

WHY Avogadro's principle (or law) says that at the same temperature and pressure, equal volumes of any gas contain the same number of particles. At STP, one mole of an ideal gas takes up about 22.4 liters.

QUESTION 2 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Based on trends in the periodic table, which of the following elements is the least electronegative?

  • W) Nitrogen
  • X) Carbon
  • Y) Beryllium
  • Z) Oxygen

ANSWER Y) Beryllium

WHY Electronegativity increases from left to right across a period. Of these period-2 elements, beryllium is the furthest left, so it is the least electronegative.

QUESTION 3 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following solid compounds would NOT be soluble to a large extent in water, but would be soluble in hydrochloric acid?

  • W) Ba(NO3)2
  • X) CuSO4
  • Y) MgCO3
  • Z) KClO4

ANSWER Y) MgCO3

WHY Magnesium carbonate barely dissolves in water, but carbonates react with acid: MgCO₃ + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂. The other three are soluble nitrate, sulfate and perchlorate salts.

QUESTION 4 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following is the formula for n-Octadecanoic acid?

  • W) C6H12O6
  • X) C18H36COOH
  • Y) C17H35COOH
  • Z) C16H32COOH

ANSWER Y) C17H35COOH

WHY Octadecanoic acid (stearic acid) has 18 carbons in total. One carbon is in the COOH group, so the rest of the chain is C₁₇H₃₅, giving C₁₇H₃₅COOH.

QUESTION 5 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following neutral ground state atoms has the lowest first ionization energy?

  • W) Nitrogen
  • X) Oxygen
  • Y) Fluorine
  • Z) Neon

ANSWER X) Oxygen

WHY Ionization energy usually rises across a period, but oxygen breaks the trend. Its fourth 2p electron is paired with another in the same orbital, and the repulsion between them makes it easier to remove than nitrogen's electrons.

QUESTION 6 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following molecules is nonpolar?

  • W) Acetone
  • X) Carbon tetrachloride
  • Y) Sulfur dioxide
  • Z) Iodine chloride

ANSWER X) Carbon tetrachloride

WHY CCl₄ has four polar C–Cl bonds, but its tetrahedral shape makes them cancel out, so the molecule has no overall dipole. SO₂ is bent, and acetone and ICl are lopsided, so all three are polar.

QUESTION 7 · SHORT ANSWER

What is the pH of a solution of hydrochloric acid that is initially 0.1 molar but is diluted 100-fold?

ANSWER 3

WHY 0.1 M HCl has [H⁺] = 10⁻¹, so its pH is 1. Diluting 100-fold makes [H⁺] = 10⁻³, so the pH becomes 3. Each 10-fold dilution of a strong acid raises the pH by 1.

QUESTION 8 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Silicon can be doped with which of the following to produce a p-type semiconductor?

  • W) Oxygen
  • X) Aluminum
  • Y) Carbon
  • Z) Phosphorus

ANSWER X) Aluminum

WHY A p-type semiconductor needs a dopant with fewer valence electrons than silicon's four. Aluminum has three, which leaves positive "holes" that carry current. Phosphorus, with five, would make an n-type semiconductor.

QUESTION 9 · SHORT ANSWER

What allotrope of sulfur contains irreducible units of S8 rings and is the most stable allotrope at room temperature and atmospheric pressure?

ANSWER Rhombic sulfur

WHY Rhombic (orthorhombic) sulfur, made of crown-shaped S₈ rings, is the stable form at room temperature. Above about 96 °C it changes to monoclinic sulfur.

QUESTION 10 · SHORT ANSWER

Rank the following three metals in order of increasing strength as a reducing agent: 1) Copper; 2) Zinc; 3) Lead

ANSWER 1, 3, 2

WHY A stronger reducing agent gives up electrons more easily. On the activity series zinc is the most active, then lead, then copper. So in increasing order: copper, lead, zinc.

QUESTION 11 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following is NOT a homogeneous mixture?

  • W) Gasoline
  • X) Air
  • Y) Smoke
  • Z) Vinegar

ANSWER Y) Smoke

WHY Smoke is solid particles spread through air, an unevenly mixed colloid, so it is heterogeneous. Gasoline, air and vinegar are all evenly mixed solutions.

QUESTION 12 · SHORT ANSWER

When calcium chloride is dissolved in water, its van't Hoff factor would be closest to what whole number?

ANSWER 3

WHY CaCl₂ splits into one Ca²⁺ ion and two Cl⁻ ions, which makes 3 particles per formula unit. So its ideal van't Hoff factor is 3.

QUESTION 13 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following best describes the kinetics of radioactive decay?

  • W) It is a zeroth order process
  • X) It is a first order process
  • Y) It is a second order process
  • Z) It is a third order process

ANSWER X) It is a first order process

WHY Radioactive decay is first order: the rate depends only on how much of the isotope is present. That's why its half-life stays constant no matter how much sample you start with.

QUESTION 14 · SHORT ANSWER

Water has a boiling point elevation constant of 0.5 degrees Celsius per molal. Assuming a van't Hoff factor of 2, how much sodium chloride would have to be added to 100 milliliters of water to create a solution with a boiling point of 100.5 degrees Celsius?

ANSWER 2.9

WHY ΔT = i·Kb·m, so 0.5 = 2 × 0.5 × m and m = 0.5 mol/kg. 100 mL of water is 0.1 kg, so you need 0.05 mol of NaCl. At 58.4 g/mol, that's about 2.9 grams.

QUESTION 15 · SHORT ANSWER

Identify all of the following three compounds that contain both covalent and ionic bonds: 1) Phosphorus oxychloride; 2) Ammonium sulfate; 3) Potassium chloride

ANSWER 2

WHY Ammonium sulfate, (NH₄)₂SO₄, has covalent bonds inside the NH₄⁺ and SO₄²⁻ ions and ionic bonds between them. POCl₃ is only covalent, and KCl is only ionic.

HIGH SCHOOL CHEMISTRY STUDY TIPS

  • Memorize the periodic trends (electronegativity, ionization energy, atomic radius) and their exceptions, like oxygen's lower ionization energy compared with nitrogen's.
  • Practice pH and dilution math without a calculator. Most questions use powers of 10 so the logs come out to whole numbers.
  • Know the activity series and solubility rules. They answer a lot of "which one reacts" and "which one dissolves" questions quickly.
