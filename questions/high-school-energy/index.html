HIGH SCHOOL ENERGY QUESTIONS

GRADES 9-12 · 15 REAL QUESTIONS · ANSWERS EXPLAINED

Energy is one of the six Science Bowl subjects. At the high school level it covers how energy is produced and stored, nuclear and renewable power, thermodynamics, and research at DOE national laboratories. Below are 15 real questions from official National Science Bowl sets, each with its answer and a short explanation of why it's right. There are 154 high school energy questions in all on this site. When you're ready, practise them against the clock or review the high school energy concepts.

QUESTION 1 · SHORT ANSWER

When rubber is vulcanized in order to make it more durable, what element is added to the rubber?

ANSWER Sulfur

WHY Vulcanization heats rubber with sulfur. The sulfur forms cross-links between polymer chains, making the rubber stronger, more elastic and less sticky. Charles Goodyear developed it in 1839.

QUESTION 2 · SHORT ANSWER

If 6 amperes of current flow through a 5 ohm resistor, what is the voltage across the resistor?

ANSWER 30

WHY Ohm's law: V = IR = 6 A × 5 Ω = 30 volts.

QUESTION 3 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

What property of hydrogen makes water a good neutron moderator?

  • W) It has a small neutron scattering cross section
  • X) It has a large neutron scattering cross section
  • Y) It is fissile
  • Z) It evaporates at 373 Kelvin

ANSWER X) It has a large neutron scattering cross section

WHY A moderator slows fast neutrons so they're more likely to cause fission. Hydrogen nuclei are about the same mass as a neutron and scatter them strongly, so each collision takes away a lot of the neutron's energy.

QUESTION 4 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following is a primary issue associated with harnessing solar power via panels?

  • W) Low efficiency
  • X) Overheating the foundation under the panels
  • Y) Difficult assembly
  • Z) Rain damage to the solar panels

ANSWER W) Low efficiency

WHY Most commercial solar panels convert only about 15–23% of sunlight into electricity, so low efficiency is the main challenge.

QUESTION 5 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following did NOT come from a once-living organism?

  • W) Gasohol
  • X) Crude oil
  • Y) Uranium
  • Z) Charcoal

ANSWER Y) Uranium

WHY Uranium is a mineral element formed in exploding stars long before Earth existed. Gasohol (with ethanol from crops), crude oil (from ancient marine life) and charcoal (from wood) all came from living things.

QUESTION 6 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following is the least geographically-restricted form of grid-scale energy storage?

  • W) Pumped hydro
  • X) Batteries
  • Y) Compressed air
  • Z) Hot water

ANSWER X) Batteries

WHY Pumped hydro needs hills and water, and compressed air needs underground caverns. Batteries can be installed almost anywhere.

QUESTION 7 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Biodiesel contains less sulfur than petroleum-based diesel. When compared to petroleum-based diesel, which of the following is true about biodiesel due to the lower amount of sulfur?

  • W) It produces more pollutants
  • X) It is a better lubricant
  • Y) It is a worse lubricant
  • Z) It has a lower flashpoint

ANSWER X) It is a better lubricant

WHY Sulfur compounds help lubricate petroleum diesel, so ultra-low-sulfur diesel lubricates engines poorly. Biodiesel still lubricates well despite its low sulfur, and it is often blended in to restore lubricity.

QUESTION 8 · SHORT ANSWER

What is the term used to describe the medium in nuclear reactors that is used to reduce the speed of fast neutrons?

ANSWER Moderator

WHY The moderator, usually water or graphite, slows fast neutrons to thermal speeds, where U-235 is much more likely to fission.

QUESTION 9 · SHORT ANSWER

Most renewable electricity generation worldwide is generated by what form of renewable energy?

ANSWER Hydroelectricity

WHY Hydroelectric power produces more electricity worldwide than any other renewable source, although wind and solar are growing faster.

QUESTION 10 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

In a nuclear reactor, the consumption of uranium fuel results in the production of fission gases. Which of the following noble gases is commonly produced?

  • W) Helium
  • X) Argon
  • Y) Xenon
  • Z) Neon

ANSWER Y) Xenon

WHY Xenon and krypton isotopes are common fission products. Xenon-135 matters a lot in reactor operation because it absorbs neutrons very strongly.

QUESTION 11 · SHORT ANSWER

What is the name of an energy storage technology in which electrical energy is stored by accelerating a rotor?

ANSWER Flywheel

WHY A flywheel stores energy as rotational kinetic energy (½Iω²) by spinning a heavy rotor very fast, then turns it back into electricity by slowing it down.

QUESTION 12 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following values refers to the maximum amount of non-expansion work that can be extracted from a closed system?

  • W) Enthalpy
  • X) Entropy
  • Y) Helmholtz free energy
  • Z) Gibbs free energy

ANSWER Z) Gibbs free energy

WHY At constant temperature and pressure, the drop in Gibbs free energy is the most non-expansion work a system can do. Helmholtz energy plays that role at constant volume.

QUESTION 13 · SHORT ANSWER

What thermodynamic potential, symbolized by the letter U, is the sum of the heat added to a system and the work done on a system by its surroundings?

ANSWER Internal energy

WHY The first law of thermodynamics says ΔU = Q + W: the change in internal energy equals the heat added plus the work done on the system.

QUESTION 14 · SHORT ANSWER

An LED has a forward voltage drop of 3.1 volts. How many LEDs can be driven in series by a 12 volt power source?

ANSWER 3

WHY Each LED drops 3.1 V. Three LEDs use 9.3 V, which a 12 V source can supply. Four would need 12.4 V, which is too much. So the answer is 3.

QUESTION 15 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following types of electromagnetic radiation possesses the greatest amount of energy per photon?

  • W) Microwave
  • X) Ultraviolet
  • Y) Infrared
  • Z) X-Ray

ANSWER Z) X-Ray

WHY Photon energy is E = hf, so higher frequency means more energy. Of these, X-rays have the highest frequency, then ultraviolet, infrared and microwaves.

HIGH SCHOOL ENERGY STUDY TIPS

  • Know how a nuclear reactor works: fuel, moderator, control rods, coolant, and common fission products like xenon.
  • Learn the main storage technologies (pumped hydro, batteries, compressed air, flywheels) and what limits each one.
  • Many questions mention research at DOE labs like Argonne, Oak Ridge or SLAC. Don't panic, because the question underneath is usually basic chemistry or physics.
