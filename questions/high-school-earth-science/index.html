HIGH SCHOOL EARTH SCIENCE QUESTIONS

GRADES 9-12 · 15 REAL QUESTIONS · ANSWERS EXPLAINED

Earth Science is one of the six Science Bowl subjects. At the high school level it covers geology, plate tectonics, minerals and rocks, meteorology, oceanography and astronomy. Below are 15 real questions from official National Science Bowl sets, each with its answer and a short explanation of why it's right. There are 194 high school earth science questions in all on this site. When you're ready, practise them against the clock or review the high school earth science concepts.

QUESTION 1 · SHORT ANSWER

Horsts and grabens are bounded by what type of fault?

ANSWER Normal

WHY Horsts (raised blocks) and grabens (dropped blocks) form where the crust is pulled apart. Tension creates normal faults, where the hanging wall slides down.

QUESTION 2 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

In the oceans, waves and surf currents are generated by:

  • W) Wind
  • X) El-Nino
  • Y) Salinity and temperature gradients
  • Z) The Intertropical Convergence Zone

ANSWER W) Wind

WHY Wind blowing across the sea surface transfers energy to the water, building waves and driving surf currents near shore.

QUESTION 3 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

A new study has found that white spruce trees on the edge of the tundra in Alaska's far north have thrived in the past 100 years. Which of the following methodologies was most likely used to determine the age of the trees?

  • W) Sclerochronology
  • X) Dendrochronology
  • Y) Radiometric dating
  • Z) Acanthochronology

ANSWER X) Dendrochronology

WHY Dendrochronology dates trees by counting and matching their yearly growth rings. Sclerochronology does the same with shells and corals.

QUESTION 4 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following will NOT help crystallize magma?

  • W) Cooling the liquid
  • X) Increasing pressure
  • Y) Removing water to raise the melting point
  • Z) Increasing temperature

ANSWER Z) Increasing temperature

WHY Magma crystallizes when it gets below its melting point. Cooling, adding pressure, or raising the melting point all help. Raising the temperature does the opposite and keeps it liquid.

QUESTION 5 · SHORT ANSWER

In what layer of the Earth's interior will S-waves never be found?

ANSWER Outer core

WHY S-waves can't travel through liquids. The outer core is liquid iron and nickel, which creates the S-wave shadow zone seismologists use as proof that it's liquid.

QUESTION 6 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following is NOT true regarding white dwarfs?

  • W) They shrink in radius as they get cooler
  • X) They do not perform nuclear fusion
  • Y) Their mass is mostly composed of carbon and oxygen
  • Z) They can have atmospheres of helium

ANSWER W) They shrink in radius as they get cooler

WHY White dwarfs don't shrink as they cool. Electron degeneracy pressure, not heat, holds them up, so their radius stays about the same as they fade.

QUESTION 7 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following surfaces has the lowest albedo?

  • W) Ice
  • X) Desert Sand
  • Y) Ocean
  • Z) Tundra

ANSWER Y) Ocean

WHY Albedo is the share of sunlight a surface reflects. Open ocean is dark and absorbs most sunlight (albedo around 0.06), while fresh ice reflects most of it.

QUESTION 8 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Ultramafic magma is slowly cooling. Which of the following minerals will crystalize out first?

  • W) Olivine
  • X) Pyroxene
  • Y) Biotite
  • Z) Quartz

ANSWER W) Olivine

WHY Bowen's reaction series says olivine crystallizes first, at the highest temperature, from mafic and ultramafic magma. Quartz comes last.

QUESTION 9 · SHORT ANSWER

Mineral spherules present on the Martian surface are thought to be evidence of earlier surface water. Of what mineral are these spherules composed?

ANSWER Hematite

WHY NASA's Opportunity rover found small hematite spheres, nicknamed "blueberries", at Meridiani Planum. Hematite usually forms in water, so they suggest Mars was once wet.

QUESTION 10 · SHORT ANSWER

Homo sapiens evolved during which geological era of the Geological Time Scale?

ANSWER Cenozoic

WHY Homo sapiens appeared about 300,000 years ago, in the Quaternary period of the Cenozoic Era, the current era, which began 66 million years ago.

QUESTION 11 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Mount Vesuvius in Italy is a typical:

  • W) Mud volcano
  • X) Caldera
  • Y) Cinder cone
  • Z) Composite volcano

ANSWER Z) Composite volcano

WHY Vesuvius is a composite volcano (stratovolcano), built from layers of lava and ash. Thick, gassy magma makes these volcanoes erupt explosively, like the eruption that buried Pompeii in 79 AD.

QUESTION 12 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Sinkholes, disappearing streams, and caves are found in which of the following types of regions?

  • W) Marshes
  • X) Glaciated alpine
  • Y) Outwash plains
  • Z) Karst topography

ANSWER Z) Karst topography

WHY Karst landscapes form where groundwater slowly dissolves soluble rock like limestone, leaving caves, sinkholes and streams that disappear underground.

QUESTION 13 · SHORT ANSWER

Identify all of the following four rocks that would be classified as foliated metamorphic rocks: 1) Gneiss; 2) Marble; 3) Quartzite; 4) Phyllite

ANSWER 1, 4

WHY Foliated rocks have minerals lined up in layers from directed pressure. Gneiss and phyllite are foliated. Marble and quartzite are non-foliated, because they're mostly a single mineral.

QUESTION 14 · SHORT ANSWER

What prominent meteor shower is active from mid-July to late-August, and is associated with Comet Swift-Tuttle?

ANSWER Perseid

WHY The Perseids peak around August 12 each year, when Earth passes through debris left by Comet Swift-Tuttle.

QUESTION 15 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

An Ekman spiral in the surface ocean is created by the combined effect of what two influences?

  • W) Wind and water upwelling
  • X) Wind and water downwelling
  • Y) Coriolis force and water downwelling
  • Z) Coriolis force and wind

ANSWER Z) Coriolis force and wind

WHY Wind pushes the surface water, and the Coriolis effect turns each deeper layer a bit more, making a spiral. Overall, the water moves about 90° from the wind direction.

HIGH SCHOOL EARTH SCIENCE STUDY TIPS

  • Learn Bowen's reaction series and the rock cycle. They explain which minerals form first and how rocks change.
  • Know the classic examples: Vesuvius for composite volcanoes, the Perseids for meteor showers, karst for caves and sinkholes.
  • Astronomy is a big part of this subject. Review stellar evolution, the planets, and how the sky moves (precession, equinoxes, coordinates).
