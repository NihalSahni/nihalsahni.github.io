HIGH SCHOOL PHYSICS QUESTIONS

GRADES 9-12 · 15 REAL QUESTIONS · ANSWERS EXPLAINED

Physics is one of the six Science Bowl subjects. At the high school level it covers mechanics, waves, electricity and magnetism, thermodynamics and modern physics, with many quick calculations. Below are 15 real questions from official National Science Bowl sets, each with its answer and a short explanation of why it's right. There are 218 high school physics questions in all on this site. When you're ready, practise them against the clock or review the high school physics concepts.

QUESTION 1 · SHORT ANSWER

What is the wavelength, in meters, of a sound wave that has a frequency of 600 hertz and a speed in a certain medium of 360 meters per second?

ANSWER 0.6

WHY Wave speed = frequency × wavelength, so wavelength = v / f = 360 / 600 = 0.6 meters.

QUESTION 2 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Assuming that 1 atmosphere = 1.01 × 10^5 pascals and the density of water is 1000 kilograms per cubic meter, at what depth of water will the absolute pressure be 10.8 times the atmospheric pressure?

  • W) 10.1 meters
  • X) 101 meters
  • Y) 1,010 meters
  • Z) 10.1 kilometers

ANSWER X) 101 meters

WHY Absolute pressure is atmospheric pressure plus water pressure, so the water must add 9.8 atm, about 9.9 × 10⁵ Pa. Using P = ρgh, h = 9.9 × 10⁵ / (1000 × 9.8) ≈ 101 meters.

QUESTION 3 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

If 15 joules of work are required to move 3.0 coulombs of charge between two points, what is the potential difference in volts between these two points?

  • W) 3
  • X) 5
  • Y) 15
  • Z) 45

ANSWER X) 5

WHY Potential difference is work per unit charge: V = W / q = 15 J / 3 C = 5 volts.

QUESTION 4 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following is closest to the amount of energy, in joules, carried by a mole of photons with a wavelength of one micrometer?

  • W) 12,000
  • X) 24,000
  • Y) 120,000
  • Z) 240,000

ANSWER Y) 120,000

WHY One photon has E = hc/λ = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴)(3 × 10⁸) / 10⁻⁶ ≈ 2 × 10⁻¹⁹ J. Multiply by Avogadro's number (6.02 × 10²³) to get about 1.2 × 10⁵ J, or 120,000 joules.

QUESTION 5 · SHORT ANSWER

What is the power radiated, in watts, by a 50 kilo-ohm resistor that is supplied with a constant 20 milli-amp direct current?

ANSWER 20

WHY P = I²R = (0.020 A)² × 50,000 Ω = 0.0004 × 50,000 = 20 watts.

QUESTION 6 · SHORT ANSWER

A basin of water is connected to two vertically-oriented pistons, one with area 30 square centimeters and the other with area 75 square centimeters. A 50-kilogram mass sits on the smaller piston. In kilograms, what mass must be placed on the larger piston to place the system in static equilibrium?

ANSWER 125

WHY Pascal's principle says the pressure is the same under both pistons, so weight divided by area must match: 50 / 30 = m / 75. That gives m = 125 kilograms.

QUESTION 7 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

An 8-kilogram and a 20-kilogram mass are on opposite sides of a 7-meter-long lever. In meters, how far should the fulcrum be placed from the 8-kilogram mass so that the two masses are balanced?

  • W) 3.5
  • X) 4
  • Y) 4.5
  • Z) 5

ANSWER Z) 5

WHY Torques balance when 8 × d = 20 × (7 − d). That gives 28d = 140, so d = 5 meters. The fulcrum sits closer to the heavier mass.

QUESTION 8 · SHORT ANSWER

You are keeping a 2-kilogram book pressed up against a wall by pushing with just enough force in the horizontal direction to prevent the book from slipping. If you are pushing with 50 newtons of force, then what is the coefficient of static friction between the book and the wall?

ANSWER 0.392

WHY Static friction has to hold up the book's weight: μN = mg. The normal force is your 50 N push, so μ = (2 × 9.8) / 50 = 0.392.

QUESTION 9 · SHORT ANSWER

If a radioactive element's half-life is 60 days, then, to the nearest whole number, how many grams of a 1-kilogram sample remain after 300 days?

ANSWER 31

WHY 300 days is 5 half-lives. 1000 g × (1/2)⁵ = 1000 / 32 = 31.25, so about 31 grams remain.

QUESTION 10 · SHORT ANSWER

Bill pulls a sled with 10 newtons of force using a rope that is angled 60 degrees above the horizontal. How much work in joules does he perform on the sled if he pulls the sled for 3 meters over the course of 4 seconds?

ANSWER 15

WHY Only the part of the force along the motion does work: W = F·d·cos θ = 10 × 3 × cos 60° = 15 joules. The 4 seconds doesn't matter, since time affects power, not work.

QUESTION 11 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

A child is holding a string with a yo-yo dangling at the bottom of it. As the child yanks the string up, the tension in the string will:

  • W) Decrease
  • X) Increase
  • Y) Stay the same
  • Z) Become negative

ANSWER X) Increase

WHY Yanking upward accelerates the yo-yo up, so the string has to pull with more than its weight: T = m(g + a). The tension increases.

QUESTION 12 · SHORT ANSWER

To the nearest microfarad, what is the capacitance of a standard parallel plate capacitor with plate separation of 1 micron and plate area of 1 meter squared?

ANSWER 9

WHY C = ε₀A / d = (8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 1) / (1 × 10⁻⁶) = 8.85 × 10⁻⁶ F, which rounds to 9 microfarads.

QUESTION 13 · SHORT ANSWER

Three resistors are connected in parallel to a 10-amp DC current source. If the individual resistances are 5 ohms, 8 ohms and 20 ohms, what is the power, to the nearest watt, dissipated by the circuit?

ANSWER 267

WHY Parallel resistors combine as 1/R = 1/5 + 1/8 + 1/20 = 0.375, so R ≈ 2.67 Ω. Then P = I²R = 10² × 2.67 ≈ 267 watts.

QUESTION 14 · SHORT ANSWER

A standing wave is produced on a string fixed at both ends. What is the number of antinodes minus the number of nodes?

ANSWER -1

WHY On a string fixed at both ends, nodes and antinodes alternate and both ends are nodes, so there is always one more node than antinode. Antinodes minus nodes is −1.

QUESTION 15 · SHORT ANSWER

A 1500-kilogram car requires the engine to provide a 4000-newton force to maintain a constant speed of 30 meters per second. In watts, what is the power that is being exerted by the engine?

ANSWER 120,000

WHY At constant speed, power = force × velocity = 4000 N × 30 m/s = 120,000 watts (120 kW).

HIGH SCHOOL PHYSICS STUDY TIPS

  • Learn the core formulas until they're automatic: v = fλ, P = IV = I²R, W = Fd cos θ, P = Fv, and V = W/q.
  • Questions are built so the numbers come out cleanly. If your answer is messy, check your units and look for a simpler setup.
  • Watch for distractors. Information like how long something took is sometimes included even when it doesn't affect the answer.
