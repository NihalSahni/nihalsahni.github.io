MIDDLE SCHOOL ENERGY QUESTIONS

GRADES 6-8 · 15 REAL QUESTIONS · ANSWERS EXPLAINED

Energy is one of the six Science Bowl subjects. At the middle school level it covers energy sources, electricity, fossil fuels and renewables, and research at DOE national laboratories. Below are 15 real questions from official National Science Bowl sets, each with its answer and a short explanation of why it's right. There are 90 middle school energy questions in all on this site. When you're ready, practise them against the clock or review the middle school energy concepts.

QUESTION 1 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following did NOT come from a once-living organism?

  • W) Gasohol
  • X) Crude oil
  • Y) Uranium
  • Z) Charcoal

ANSWER Y) Uranium

WHY Uranium is a mineral element made in stars long before life existed. The other three come from living things: crops for gasohol, ancient sea life for crude oil, and wood for charcoal.

QUESTION 2 · SHORT ANSWER

Petroleum is a non-renewable resource. What term refers to the point in the future when the maximum rate of petroleum extraction will be reached, and supplies will begin to decrease?

ANSWER Peak oil

WHY Peak oil is the point when world oil production hits its highest rate. After that, production falls because the reserves get harder to reach.

QUESTION 3 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

What happens as the activation energy of a reaction increases?

  • W) The reaction rate increases
  • X) The reaction rate decreases
  • Y) The products have lower energy
  • Z) The reactants have lower energy

ANSWER X) The reaction rate decreases

WHY Activation energy is the "hill" reactants must climb before they can react. A higher hill means fewer collisions have enough energy, so the reaction slows down.

QUESTION 4 · SHORT ANSWER

A weight is suspended from a string. You pull back on the weight and release it. Identify all of the following three factors that will affect the period of the resulting motion: 1) acceleration of gravity, 2) length of the string, 3) mass of the weight

ANSWER 1, 2

WHY A pendulum's period is T = 2π√(L/g), so it depends only on string length and gravity. Changing the mass doesn't change the period.

QUESTION 5 · SHORT ANSWER

Scientists at Ames Lab are studying the properties of superconductors at temperatures of around 50 milli-kelvins. To the nearest degree, what temperature in Celsius does this equal?

ANSWER -273

WHY 0 kelvin is −273.15 °C, and 50 millikelvins is only 0.05 K, so it's about −273.1 °C. To the nearest degree: −273 °C.

QUESTION 6 · SHORT ANSWER

What is the Earth's most abundant energy source?

ANSWER The Sun

WHY Almost all of Earth's energy comes from the Sun. It drives weather, wind and the water cycle, and plants capture it through photosynthesis. Even fossil fuels are stored ancient sunlight.

QUESTION 7 · SHORT ANSWER

Identify all of the following three units that correspond to energy: 1) Watts; 2) Joules; 3) Kilowatt-hours

ANSWER 2 and 3

WHY Joules and kilowatt-hours measure energy (1 kWh = 3.6 million J). A watt measures power, which is how fast energy is used: 1 watt = 1 joule per second.

QUESTION 8 · SHORT ANSWER

What method is currently used to produce most of the world's renewable energy?

ANSWER Hydropower

WHY Hydropower makes more of the world's renewable electricity than any other source, using flowing water to spin turbines.

QUESTION 9 · SHORT ANSWER

If a transformer has an input voltage of 440 volts and an output voltage of 110 volts, what is the ratio of the number of turns in the input coil to the number of turns in the output coil?

ANSWER 4 to 1

WHY A transformer's voltage ratio equals its turns ratio: 440 V / 110 V = 4. So the input coil has 4 turns for every 1 on the output coil, a step-down transformer.

QUESTION 10 · SHORT ANSWER

Which of the three main rock types contains most of the fossil fuels we use as energy resources today?

ANSWER Sedimentary rock

WHY Coal, oil and natural gas form from buried plant and animal remains in layers of sediment, so they're found in sedimentary rock.

QUESTION 11 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following types of battery is most commonly used in automobiles?

  • W) Lithium-ion
  • X) Nickel cadmium
  • Y) Lead-acid
  • Z) Zinc chloride

ANSWER Y) Lead-acid

WHY Cars use lead-acid batteries because they're cheap and can deliver the burst of current needed to start the engine.

QUESTION 12 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Concentrated solar power stores the sun's radiation in what form of energy?

  • W) Thermal
  • X) Chemical
  • Y) Kinetic
  • Z) Acoustic

ANSWER W) Thermal

WHY Concentrated solar power uses mirrors to focus sunlight and make heat, often stored in molten salt. That heat makes steam to run a turbine, even after sunset.

QUESTION 13 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

What thermal property of home insulation does the R-value represent?

  • W) Transmissibility
  • X) Resistance per unit area
  • Y) Flux resistance
  • Z) Insolance per unit area

ANSWER X) Resistance per unit area

WHY R-value measures how well insulation resists heat flow through a given area. The higher the R-value, the better it insulates.

QUESTION 14 · SHORT ANSWER

How many kilowatt-hours, rounded to the nearest tenth, does it require to light a 100-watt bulb for 1 hour?

ANSWER 0.1

WHY Energy = power × time = 100 W × 1 h = 100 watt-hours = 0.1 kilowatt-hours.

QUESTION 15 · SHORT ANSWER

The energy stored in a uranium atom can be released via what type of nuclear reaction?

ANSWER Fission

WHY Nuclear fission splits a heavy uranium nucleus into smaller ones and releases a lot of energy. Nuclear power plants run on fission.

MIDDLE SCHOOL ENERGY STUDY TIPS

  • Know the difference between energy (joules, kilowatt-hours) and power (watts), and how to convert between them.
  • Learn the main renewable and nonrenewable sources, plus one fact about each, like hydropower being the biggest renewable.
  • Questions about DOE labs often hide a simple science question. Read to the end before you panic.
