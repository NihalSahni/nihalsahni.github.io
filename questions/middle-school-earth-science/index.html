MIDDLE SCHOOL EARTH SCIENCE QUESTIONS

GRADES 6-8 · 15 REAL QUESTIONS · ANSWERS EXPLAINED

Earth Science is one of the six Science Bowl subjects. At the middle school level it covers rocks and minerals, Earth's layers, weather, oceans, the Moon, and the solar system. Below are 15 real questions from official National Science Bowl sets, each with its answer and a short explanation of why it's right. There are 284 middle school earth science questions in all on this site. When you're ready, practise them against the clock or review the middle school earth science concepts.

QUESTION 1 · SHORT ANSWER

Identify all of the following three phases of the moon during which the tidal range is at a maximum: 1) New moon; 2) First quarter; 3) Full moon

ANSWER 1 and 3

WHY At new and full moon, the Sun, Moon and Earth line up, and their pull combines to make spring tides, with the highest highs and lowest lows.

QUESTION 2 · SHORT ANSWER

What terrestrial planet rotates in an opposite direction to the planet Earth?

ANSWER Venus

WHY Venus spins backward compared with most planets (retrograde rotation), so the Sun rises in the west there. A single Venus day lasts longer than its year.

QUESTION 3 · SHORT ANSWER

What is the name given to ground that is continuously frozen for at least two years?

ANSWER Permafrost

WHY Permafrost is ground that stays at or below 0 °C for at least two years in a row. It covers large areas of the Arctic, and thawing permafrost releases greenhouse gases.

QUESTION 4 · SHORT ANSWER

What is the name for the apparent force that causes hurricanes to be deflected to the right in the Northern hemisphere and to the left in the Southern hemisphere?

ANSWER The Coriolis effect

WHY Earth's rotation makes moving air and water appear to curve. This Coriolis effect turns them right in the Northern Hemisphere and left in the Southern.

QUESTION 5 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

A lunar eclipse will occur when which of the following happens?

  • W) The moon blocks light from the sun
  • X) The side of the moon facing Earth is completely dark
  • Y) The full moon moves through Earth's shadow
  • Z) The moon is fully lit by the sun

ANSWER Y) The full moon moves through Earth's shadow

WHY A lunar eclipse happens at full moon, when Earth is directly between the Sun and Moon and Earth's shadow falls on the Moon.

QUESTION 6 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

What type of telescope uses convex lenses to bend light?

  • W) Radio telescope
  • X) Refracting telescope
  • Y) Reflecting telescope
  • Z) Hubble telescope

ANSWER X) Refracting telescope

WHY Refracting telescopes bend light with convex lenses. Reflecting telescopes use curved mirrors instead.

QUESTION 7 · SHORT ANSWER

Identify all of the following three planets that are considered Jovian: 1) Jupiter; 2) Uranus; 3) Saturn

ANSWER All

WHY The Jovian (gas giant) planets are Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune: large planets made mostly of gases and ices.

QUESTION 8 · SHORT ANSWER

What planet in our solar system has the lowest density?

ANSWER Saturn

WHY Saturn's average density is about 0.69 g/cm³, less than water's, so in theory it would float in a big enough bathtub.

QUESTION 9 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following types of maps would be most useful on a hiking trip through the Alps?

  • W) Synoptic
  • X) Topographic
  • Y) Planimetric
  • Z) Mercator projection

ANSWER X) Topographic

WHY Topographic maps use contour lines to show elevation and steepness, which is exactly what you need for hiking in mountains.

QUESTION 10 · SHORT ANSWER

What is the name of the process in which sediments compact under pressure and gradually become solid rock?

ANSWER Lithification

WHY Lithification is how loose sediment turns into sedimentary rock: layers pile up, compact under pressure, and minerals cement the grains together.

QUESTION 11 · SHORT ANSWER

The lithosphere is made up of the uppermost mantle and what other layer?

ANSWER Crust

WHY The lithosphere is the crust plus the rigid top of the mantle. It's broken into the tectonic plates.

QUESTION 12 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Metamorphism transforms limestone into a metamorphic rock called:

  • W) Quartzite
  • X) Marble
  • Y) Hornfel
  • Z) Granite

ANSWER X) Marble

WHY Heat and pressure recrystallize the calcite in limestone into marble, a non-foliated metamorphic rock.

QUESTION 13 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following is NOT a carbonate?

  • W) Dolomite
  • X) Aragonite
  • Y) Malachite
  • Z) Labradorite

ANSWER Z) Labradorite

WHY Carbonates contain the CO₃ group. Dolomite, aragonite and malachite are carbonates. Labradorite is a feldspar, which is a silicate mineral.

QUESTION 14 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

The aurora borealis, or northern lights, would not be observed if Earth lacked which of the following?

  • W) Oxygen in its atmosphere
  • X) A moon
  • Y) A magnetic field
  • Z) High altitude clouds

ANSWER Y) A magnetic field

WHY Earth's magnetic field funnels charged particles from the solar wind toward the poles. When they hit the upper atmosphere, it glows. Without the field, there would be no auroras.

QUESTION 15 · MULTIPLE CHOICE

Which of the following atmospheric layers contains the ozone layer?

  • W) Mesosphere
  • X) Thermosphere
  • Y) Stratosphere
  • Z) Troposphere

ANSWER Y) Stratosphere

WHY The ozone layer is in the stratosphere, about 15–35 km up. It absorbs most of the Sun's harmful ultraviolet radiation.

MIDDLE SCHOOL EARTH SCIENCE STUDY TIPS

  • Learn the planets' key facts: order, size, density, and oddities like Venus's backward spin.
  • Know the rock cycle and the three rock types, including which rocks turn into which (limestone into marble, shale into slate).
  • Review Moon phases, eclipses and tides together. They all come from how the Sun, Earth and Moon line up.
